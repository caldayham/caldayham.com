Capitalism Jesus

2026-07-18

I believe the human race is facing a massive economic crisis soon. I'm not referring to the friction and subsequent price hikes our current president, Trump, has imposed with his tariffs; it's far more serious, with deeper implications for the future of humanity than that.

The crisis I'm pointing at is a trust crisis. Trust is the real currency underneath the money — it's what makes cheap, fast transactions between strangers possible at all — and right now it's being drained faster than it's being minted. Every scam, every dark-pattern subscription designed to be easy to start and impossible to cancel, every dropship markup on garbage is a small withdrawal from a commons that took generations to fill. And AI is about to collapse the cost of producing convincing-but-hollow value down to nearly zero: soon anyone will be able to generate a plausible storefront, a plausible review, a plausible person. The world of advertising is going to become a bloodbath, and I think it hits a wall fairly abruptly. When it does, people will have to re-learn how to market properly, and before they can do that they'll have to re-learn how to actually make a good product — which is so difficult that many of them may simply collapse in on their own over-consumption habits instead.

We need a capitalist who essentially makes so much money that he doesn't need to price gouge. He can assemble all the wealth he needs to make changes in the world without further eroding our trust in each other. In fact, the ratio of value he brings to the human race vs. the alpha he skims off those transactions will be so favorable to the consumer that it will actually rebuild trust.

Before going further I want to be clear about something: I am not claiming to be capitalism Jesus, and I don't want anyone walking away from this article thinking that's what it says. I try to walk in his footsteps every day, the way I feel he would walk — and the entire point of publishing this is to get others walking in those footsteps too, so that I'm not alone out here. Capitalism Jesus is probably not a single person. But he might be. Either way, the pillars below aren't a description of a savior we're waiting on; they're instructions anyone running a business can start following today.

Here is an example: you know two men, Jerry and Tom. Both of them are interested in cars and want to become mechanics. Jerry was exposed to drugs like nicotine, alcohol, weed, and cocaine as a youth, by no real fault of his own, and while he still holds onto a love for cars, he spends most of his weekends on benders with degenerates before coming back into the shop on Monday hungover. Tom, on the other hand, is spiraling upwards. By no fault of his own, he was entered into a go-kart building competition in his youth and won his first-ever race in a car he had poured hours of effort into. Unknown to Tom, one of his opponents, who was more experienced and had a truly better kart, ran into a technical issue and otherwise would have won the race. Tom's kart was great, but even great things sometimes need a little circumstance to be extraordinary. Either way, both men started from the same place — a love of cars — and the gap between them was mostly circumstance compounding on itself, one lucky race stacking confidence on confidence while one early exposure stacked the other direction. A capitalism Jesus doesn't get to pretend the race was fair before he arrived; he builds knowing his customers and his competitors are all somewhere on one of these two spirals, and that every transaction either feeds trust into the flywheel or siphons it out.

The three pillars of capitalism Jesus followers

A/V ratio less than or equal to 1 (lower the better)

This goes back a little bit to my video on wanting to re-instill shame in people running businesses with high alpha-to-value ratios — for example, drop shipping.

You should shoot for an alpha-to-value ratio of 1 if you want to be morally neutral, and under 1 if you want to be building societal trust and goodwill. If your A/V ratio is 1, that means for every dollar of profit you net, you've created a dollar of new, unique value.

There's a deeper reason to hold your A/V ratio down, and it connects back to Tom and Jerry: if your success is part circumstance — and it always is — then some of your alpha was never really yours. It was a subsidy from luck. Keeping your ratio at or under 1 is how you pay the luck subsidy back to the system that issued it.

Sell a society-advancing product, not society-degrading

Avoid businesses that sell products that actively degrade society and make people less capable, such as gambling businesses, vape & alcohol companies, pornography companies, etc.

Don't cripple the crippled

The final piece of the puzzle you need to consider is pricing based on societal needs. If you're a drug company that has just spent millions developing a new gene therapy treatment for Alzheimer's that only your company knows how to do, you have created a MASSIVE amount of value. In fact, the value you have created is basically whatever a customer is willing to pay, and for a disease like Alzheimer's it's probably quite a bit.

Plus, it checks off the second pillar of capitalism Jesus, because it is very clear that, given there are no detrimental side effects, an Alzheimer's cure is quite a positive product to sell society.

But all that said, you may still have a weird feeling in your gut about a company charging, say, 95% of a person's net worth (or more with loans) to cure or treat their Alzheimer's because they have no other choice.

This is a very complex issue because almost every product lies on this spectrum of being needed, even if it's just a set of diapers. The thing with diapers, though, is that they have already been arbitraged down to very affordable market rates due to the simplicity of manufacturing and distributing them.

Pricing something like life-saving drugs that may take cents to produce (after research costs are paid off) and could sell for thousands is a very difficult problem. You don't want to take someone who is already struggling with a crippling problem and just shift their problem over to a financially crippling one — or maybe you do; at least that's better than nearly dying, I'd assume.

I think for now the best way to deal with this one is the "smell test": does insulin selling for thousands a month in the US make sense when you can buy it for 5x cheaper across the Mexican border? I don't know, smells a little fishy to me. I don't think capitalism Jesus would run his business like that.

And I'll own what the smell test really is: an admission that no formula exists for this pillar, and none ever will. The first two pillars you can calculate. This one comes down to the character of the person doing the pricing — which is exactly why the world needs people deliberately practicing this, and not just rules on paper.

I also know that it's unreasonable to please everyone. Some people are going to be upset if they know you have ANY profit margin, but profits are necessary and good.

This makes the absolute ideal a business whose margin nobody sane resents — one that profits enough to keep compounding, but creates so much more value than it captures that the profit reads as a receipt for trust earned rather than alpha skimmed. Under that lens, profit stops being the thing you apologize for and becomes the proof that you're playing the long game with society instead of against it.

Notice that nowhere in this article have I asked for a regulation. The enforcement mechanism I'm proposing is shame — and shame is free, decentralized, and scales with culture instead of law. That's why this last part isn't a stray thought; it's the foundation the whole thing stands on:

The reason we have those twinges of shame when we do things like lie or cheat or steal is because, evolutionarily, societies whose citizens prioritized their immediate desires at the expense of long-term stability (aka lying, cheating, stealing, rape, etc.) actually did far worse than those made up of people who were slightly more altruistic.

if you are reading this and you are a real human, just know that I wrote this, as myself, in the past, at an earlier date than you are hashed into, and for that, I am sorry.
